# Publication and student review checklist

Completion is evidenced by actual files and working links, not by ticking a template in advance.

- Read each source passage used in a claim and compare media transcript segments to the recording, especially names, scores, dates and category labels.
- Compare three accepted and three rejected/deferred decisions with your own judgment; write your reasons and any corrections. Preserve the original policy, your revision, and both versions' actual outputs.
- Confirm each article has a distinct central argument, at least three independent producers, cross-source reasoning and a meaningful limitation. Do not treat repeated coverage of one result as independent corroboration of a causal explanation.
- Check 600–900 article words excluding references and approximately 3–5 minutes of narration. Resolve claim IDs into understandable citations and timestamped links. Identify AI-assisted drafting/presentation accurately.
- Read review.json; resolve blocking errors. Supply approval.json yourself only after reviewing the matching files. Run `bash run.sh --package runs/RUN_ID` to verify that the approved material is unchanged.
- Produce the three audience videos in Google Vids or HeyGen, review visuals and pronunciation, and upload them public or unlisted to YouTube.
- Publish the three Substack articles with free shareable access. Add each paired YouTube video to its article. Put article and source links in the video description.
- Enter actual URLs in package/publication_links.json and SUBMISSION.md. Verify course-staff access in a signed-out browser.
- Complete a second live run on a different UTC day during the assignment week. Preserve its artifacts and explain updates, duplicates, exclusions and failures.
- Record the separate 4–6 minute technical demo using actual artifacts. Complete the reflection based on what the system really did.
- Upload repository changes and retained assets/run records to your GitHub fork. Exclude credentials, virtual environment, downloaded model weights and large original media; document how media can be retrieved.
